# 04 — Four ways around NP-hardness

Rong Ge asks the class how one usually handles NP-hard problems; a student answers "some sort of approximation" [`22:21`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VuM1WnwEMRQ&t=1341), [`23:07`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VuM1WnwEMRQ&t=1387). That opens a survey of four options.

## 1. Approximation algorithms

- Sometimes genuinely fine: for well-separated k-means data, even a 2- or 3-approximation gives a reasonable clustering [`23:29`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VuM1WnwEMRQ&t=1409).
- **But it fails for maximum likelihood**, because the objective is a sum of **log** probabilities (a negative number) [`23:29`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VuM1WnwEMRQ&t=1409), [`24:06`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VuM1WnwEMRQ&t=1446).
- The killer detail: **a constant factor in log-probability space is not a constant factor in probability space** — if two logs are within a constant factor, their exponentials need not be, so the recovered `θ` can assign vastly lower probability than the optimum [`24:06`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VuM1WnwEMRQ&t=1446), [`24:29`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VuM1WnwEMRQ&t=1469).

## 2. Average-case analysis

- Solving the worst case would be ideal, but that only happens for very special problems [`24:53`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VuM1WnwEMRQ&t=1493).
- Being able to solve the **average case** — data actually drawn from `D(θ)` — is already a satisfying result, and potentially much easier [`24:53`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VuM1WnwEMRQ&t=1493), [`25:21`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VuM1WnwEMRQ&t=1521).
- Realistically one may want something **in between**: data roughly generated by `D(θ)` [`25:21`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VuM1WnwEMRQ&t=1521).
- Average case can also cover randomness in the parameters themselves — e.g. assuming Gaussian means are random vectors, which further simplifies the problem [`25:47`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VuM1WnwEMRQ&t=1547), [`26:08`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VuM1WnwEMRQ&t=1568).

## 3. Natural assumptions — the lecture's chosen route

- Abstract models like mixtures of Gaussians apply in many settings, but in a **specific real system** the parameters carry meaning, and that meaning yields extra restrictions enabling faster algorithms [`26:08`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VuM1WnwEMRQ&t=1568), [`26:41`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VuM1WnwEMRQ&t=1601).
- This is the route the rest of the lecture takes for topic models [`26:08`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VuM1WnwEMRQ&t=1568).

## 4. Heuristics

- If nothing else works, use a heuristic and hope it does well in practice [`26:41`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VuM1WnwEMRQ&t=1601).

## The synthesis

- **These options are not mutually exclusive.** Using a heuristic should not stop you from analyzing it under average-case or natural-assumption models [`27:25`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VuM1WnwEMRQ&t=1645).
- The stated research goal: *a heuristic algorithm that works in practice, which we can also analyze* under approximation, average case, or natural assumptions [`27:25`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VuM1WnwEMRQ&t=1645), [`28:24`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VuM1WnwEMRQ&t=1704).
- Status of the field: achieved for only a small set of problems; a relatively new area with many open problems [`28:24`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VuM1WnwEMRQ&t=1704), [`29:20`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VuM1WnwEMRQ&t=1760).
- What today delivers instead: an algorithm **different from the existing heuristics**, analyzable under natural assumptions, for learning topic models [`29:20`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VuM1WnwEMRQ&t=1760).
