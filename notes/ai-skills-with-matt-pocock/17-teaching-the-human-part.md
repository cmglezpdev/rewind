# Teaching: the human part

- The host asks how software education changed as tutorials, video courses, and AI tools shifted how developers learn. [`1:24:21`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=5061)
- Matt says video tutorials took off around COVID because learners wanted a richer learning experience. [`1:24:47`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=5087)
- He argues that learners still want curation, even if agents can explain individual topics. [`1:25:12`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=5112)
- Matt sees teaching as turning a dependency graph of information into a sensible linear path. [`1:25:12`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=5112)
- He calls that sequencing work strategic and says AI is not especially good at it yet. [`1:25:38`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=5138)
- His own education business moved from tactical TypeScript material toward strategic AI-agent workflow material. [`1:25:38`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=5138)
- Matt says the industry shifted in about seven months, changing what people value and what educators should focus on. [`1:26:08`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=5168)
- He resists turning his pivot into a tidy success story and credits luck, timing, and persistence after several failed attempts with AI. [`1:27:52`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=5272)
