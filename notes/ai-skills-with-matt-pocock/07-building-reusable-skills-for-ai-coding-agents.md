# Building reusable skills for AI coding agents

- Matt defines skills as folders of Markdown files that agents or users can invoke to change an agent's behavior. [`30:33`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=1833)
- He saw other ecosystems distribute similar behavior through skill sets, plugins, or command-style workflows. [`30:58`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=1858)
- His own skills repo gained more stars than anything else he had made before he put much promotion behind it. [`30:58`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=1858)
- A talk titled "Software Fundamentals Still Matter" reached about 1.2 million views, and the skills repo reached about 230,000 stars. [`31:25`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=1885)
- He describes the skills as his own workflow packaged in the simplest form people can inspect and adapt. [`32:15`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=1935)
- Grill Me asks the agent to interview the user relentlessly before implementation. [`34:21`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=2061)
- The host's Grill Me example forced decisions about authentication shape, safety, and rate limiting for a simple subscriber-check API. [`33:09`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=1989)
- Matt sees Grill Me as reproducing the feel of a senior engineer asking sharp questions before the work starts. [`34:50`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=2090)
- The skill improves output by improving the quality of the conversation between human and agent. [`35:39`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=2139)
- Grill Me keeps key decisions with the human while still surfacing details the human may have missed. [`36:08`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=2168)
- Matt warns that agents cannot read a developer's values, so the workflow must communicate what is in scope, out of scope, and important. [`37:11`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=2231)
