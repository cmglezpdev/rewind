# AI's impact on technical education

- The host frames AI as a direct challenge to online educators because learners may ask bots instead of buying courses. [`23:22`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=1402)
- Matt separates teaching into "what" knowledge, such as syntax, and "why" wisdom, such as judgment about when and how to use it. [`23:57`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=1437)
- He argues that knowledge has become cheap to acquire, but wisdom has not become easier to learn. [`24:24`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=1464)
- Total TypeScript revenue went down because people became less interested in that kind of tactical material. [`25:02`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=1502)
- Matt first tried making material about putting AI into applications, but later concluded it was the wrong bet for him. [`25:31`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=1531)
- Around the winter break after December, he saw that agents had become good enough to delegate real work to. [`25:58`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=1558)
- His view is that AI has largely eaten tactical programming, leaving humans responsible for strategic programming. [`26:22`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=1582)
- Experience building agent applications helped him think about data flow, state, prompt placement, compaction, and how agents pass information between steps. [`28:24`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=1704)
- He began treating agent workflows as processes, loops, or finite state machines that move information through multiple steps. [`29:08`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=1748)
- Skills became his preferred distribution mechanism for sharing those workflows. [`30:00`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=1800)
