# Gardeners and great engineers

- The host introduces the "gardener" metaphor: someone watches incoming PRs, notices smells, and removes weeds before they overtake the codebase. [`1:31:07`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=5467)
- Matt says engineers are becoming their agents' platform team, responsible for the environment where agents succeed. [`1:31:32`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=5492)
- Gardening means diagnosing entropy before it becomes a larger codebase problem. [`1:31:52`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=5512)
- Matt sees agent-driven codebase improvement from bug reports and feedback as valuable, interesting engineering work. [`1:32:20`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=5540)
- He points to Lars Grammel's work on a software factory for the Vercel AI SDK as an example of high-level engineering around process and tooling. [`1:32:45`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=5565)
- The key trait Matt names for great engineers is introspection: observing one's process and turning it into something an AI can use. [`1:33:14`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=5594)
- He values the ability to put human process into words, then encode that process into skills and automations. [`1:33:14`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=5594)
