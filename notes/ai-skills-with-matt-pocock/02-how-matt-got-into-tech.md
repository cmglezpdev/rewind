# How Matt got into tech

- Matt taught himself development so he could work remotely outside London. [`5:48`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=348)
- His first software projects came from improving his own voice lessons, including flashcard tools and a web audio analyzer for vocal resonance. [`5:48`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=348)
- The web audio analyzer was ambitious, technically rough, and useful enough to improve lessons. [`6:14`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=374)
- He entered the job market around 2017 with some JavaScript, Sass, and self-taught frontend experience. [`6:39`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=399)
- His voice-coaching background helped him interview well and explain technical ideas before he had deep technical experience. [`7:01`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=421)
- Early jobs exposed him to small-company engineering, Linux-heavy environments, and agency work. [`7:59`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=479)
- TypeScript became important to him on an agency project where frontend bugs and changing backend contracts slowed the team. [`9:14`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=554)
- After adopting TypeScript, Matt says the frontend team's velocity rose enough that the organization moved people off the team because it was moving so quickly. [`9:39`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=579)
