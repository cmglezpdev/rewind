# Shared limits and Theo's final recommendation

## What now shares the allowance

- ChatGPT and Work now count against the same limits as Codex; previously their usage was separate, according to Theo. Heavy users of those products therefore need to consider their combined consumption. [`13:49`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hq2c7pww1Ds&t=829)
- Theo expects ordinary chat and Work usage to consume much less than daily coding-agent use, so he thinks the doubling should cover typical combined usage. This is his expectation, not a measured result presented in the video. [`13:49`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hq2c7pww1Ds&t=829) [`14:16`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hq2c7pww1Ds&t=856)
- He initially says Dots remains outside the shared limits, then immediately admits uncertainty. The video does not establish Dots' actual billing treatment. [`14:16`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hq2c7pww1Ds&t=856) [`14:40`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hq2c7pww1Ds&t=880)

## How Theo chooses

- For maximum review throughput, he presents Soul as an option for people willing to accept lower preferred-review quality for roughly 30–50% more reviews under his estimates. For the best model, he favors Opus and argues Claude's subscription value is closer to Codex than the API prices suggest. [`12:58`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hq2c7pww1Ds&t=778) [`13:25`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hq2c7pww1Ds&t=805)
- His own division of work is OpenAI for computer use and verification, Claude for daily coding, and Grockbot through a separate subscription. [`14:40`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hq2c7pww1Ds&t=880)
- He welcomes the limit increase, while stressing that the $200 change restores the previous allowance. His remaining objection is model quality: he wants an OpenAI release that competes directly with Opus 5.5. [`15:01`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hq2c7pww1Ds&t=901) [`15:28`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hq2c7pww1Ds&t=928)
