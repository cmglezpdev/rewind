# Final model choices and limits of the verdict

## Theo's decisions

- He rejects Gemini and standard Muse Wonder as sources of useful T3 code contributions, while explicitly distinguishing Muse Wonderful Full. He proposes automatically closing PRs made with the rejected models; this is an intention, not a policy shown as implemented. [`36:30`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NE006pkiAnk&t=2190)
- He says reviewing Gemini's code cost more time and tokens than using a stronger model from the start. His remaining Gemini uses are YouTube data retrieval and possibly an early pass on an attractive marketing page. [`37:06`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NE006pkiAnk&t=2226)
- He reports merging none of Gemini's output from this testing. His closing preference is primarily Opus 5.5, despite joking that he will stick with Muse. [`37:30`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NE006pkiAnk&t=2250) [`38:14`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NE006pkiAnk&t=2294)

## What the review leaves open

- Theo does not test the limits of the $200 subscription plan. His comment that usage might feel effectively unlimited is frustration, not a measured quota result. [`36:12`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NE006pkiAnk&t=2172)
- Specialized vision and multilingual work remain incompletely tested; the favorable Skatebench result is concrete, while broader specialist recommendations require task-specific evaluation. [`9:47`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NE006pkiAnk&t=587) [`16:10`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NE006pkiAnk&t=970) [`16:37`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NE006pkiAnk&t=997)

- His conclusion rests on his private audits, game demonstration, implementation experience, and workflow preferences. It supports a strong negative judgment about his code work while retaining the specific exceptions documented elsewhere in the notes.
