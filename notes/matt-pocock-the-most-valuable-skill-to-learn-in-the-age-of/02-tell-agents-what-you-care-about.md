# Tell agents what you care about

## Make priorities explicit

- Pocock argues that the human-agent communication gap persists regardless of model intelligence. Explain the desired outcome and the qualities you care about rather than assuming the agent will infer them. [`4:24`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z8lf5oWKfj0&t=264)
- The codebase influences subsequent output. In his account, a neglected codebase encourages more poor code, while caring about the working environment improves what agents can produce. [`5:02`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z8lf5oWKfj0&t=302)

## Raise the minimum quality of work

1. Think like someone managing 100 developers: ask what would improve the work of the weakest contributor. This is Pocock's analogy for designing an agent's environment. [`5:27`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z8lf5oWKfj0&t=327)
2. Learn established engineering practices, especially how to write useful tests and checks that prevent recurring classes of bugs. Pocock sees these older ideas as directly applicable to agents. [`5:56`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z8lf5oWKfj0&t=356) [`6:16`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z8lf5oWKfj0&t=376)
3. Make those quality priorities part of the conversation. Agents can help teach the practices when you ask informed questions and show interest in learning them. [`6:37`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z8lf5oWKfj0&t=397)
