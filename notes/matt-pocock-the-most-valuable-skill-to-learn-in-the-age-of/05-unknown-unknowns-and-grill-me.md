# Expose unknown unknowns with Grill Me

## Why intent alone is incomplete

- Pocock says developers and non-developers both struggle to specify exactly what they want. The problem includes missing requirements that the requester does not yet recognize. [`16:04`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z8lf5oWKfj0&t=964) [`16:30`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z8lf5oWKfj0&t=990)
- Unmentioned concerns can lead an agent to invent an answer or treat a concern as unimportant. Security and deployment are his concrete examples. [`17:34`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z8lf5oWKfj0&t=1054)
- Some weaknesses only become apparent when an idea becomes executable code, because vague intentions must become connected, precise statements. Interviewing cannot eliminate all design discovery. [`18:04`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z8lf5oWKfj0&t=1084) [`18:35`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z8lf5oWKfj0&t=1115)

## What Grill Me does

1. Have the agent interview you relentlessly about the proposed product. [`17:01`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z8lf5oWKfj0&t=1021)
2. Use the questions to explain your priorities and reveal issues you had not considered. [`17:01`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z8lf5oWKfj0&t=1021)
3. Remain willing to revise the design as implementation exposes gaps. The underlying skill is recognizing that your design may be incomplete. [`18:04`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z8lf5oWKfj0&t=1084) [`18:35`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z8lf5oWKfj0&t=1115)

## Carry uncertainty into production

- Herk describes telling clients that protections cannot anticipate every real-world input. He pairs that warning with monitoring and a commitment to keep failures safe. This is his operational example, rather than a claim that upfront planning covers every edge case. [`18:59`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z8lf5oWKfj0&t=1139) [`19:23`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z8lf5oWKfj0&t=1163)
