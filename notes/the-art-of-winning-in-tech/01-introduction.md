# Introduction: the four-rule career framework

- Khader frames the problem as a difficult tech job market and pressure from AI. His thesis is that people can use AI as a career advantage by changing how they learn, share work, and execute tasks, including when they have little experience. [`0:00`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j7vSphawXyY&t=0)
- The four rules are to seek people you can learn from, make your work visible, automate repetitive tasks, and direct AI through a structured workflow. [`0:31`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j7vSphawXyY&t=31) [`2:40`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j7vSphawXyY&t=160) [`5:57`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j7vSphawXyY&t=357) [`9:02`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j7vSphawXyY&t=542)

## How to read the advice

- The learning and automation arguments draw on Khader's own conference and workplace experiences. Treat these as examples supporting his recommendations. [`0:31`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j7vSphawXyY&t=31) [`5:57`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j7vSphawXyY&t=357)
- The business and recruiting examples are stories Khader reports about other people. Their results illustrate his argument for visibility. [`3:34`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j7vSphawXyY&t=214) [`4:28`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j7vSphawXyY&t=268)
