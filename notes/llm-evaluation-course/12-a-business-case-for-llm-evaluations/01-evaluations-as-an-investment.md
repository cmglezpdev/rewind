# 01 — Evaluations as an investment

## Framing

- Building an LLM evaluation framework is explicitly an investment: it costs time and money, so it needs a business justification. [`0:01`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZaOo2dce20A&t=1)
- When you build an AI product you are already investing — in cloud costs, infrastructure, and manpower — and you do it for a reason: to save money or to earn money. [`0:16`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZaOo2dce20A&t=16)
- Concrete example: an LLM support chatbot might be expected to cut support costs in half; evaluations are how you verify that this investment actually pays off. [`0:16`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZaOo2dce20A&t=16)

## The oversight analogy

- Evaluations are compared to established oversight practices: safety testing for manufactured products, quality assurance for software, performance reviews for employees. [`0:37`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZaOo2dce20A&t=37)
- Core principle: "if you have an asset, you must invest in the oversight". [`0:37`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZaOo2dce20A&t=37)

## The four business reasons (roadmap of the video)

1. Quality
2. Safety
3. Compliance
4. Efficiency
