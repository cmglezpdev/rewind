# 05 — Efficiency

## Costs of NOT having evaluations

- The team simply slows down: iterations take longer when you cannot objectively measure whether a change improves things. [`2:20`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZaOo2dce20A&t=140)
- Shipping updates becomes stressful because you don't know what they're breaking. [`2:20`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZaOo2dce20A&t=140)
- You cannot migrate to a new or cheaper model — called out as "a very real risk". [`2:20`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZaOo2dce20A&t=140)
- Providers have already discontinued support for models, forcing switches — impossible to do safely without proper evaluations in place. [`2:37`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZaOo2dce20A&t=157)
- Debugging becomes a nightmare: when someone reports an issue you have no way to trace what caused it, nor to test that you actually fixed it. [`2:37`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZaOo2dce20A&t=157)

## The flip side

- A good evaluation system is a competitive advantage: the team moves quicker, tries new ideas faster, makes changes faster, and can be first to deliver features to the market. [`2:55`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZaOo2dce20A&t=175)
