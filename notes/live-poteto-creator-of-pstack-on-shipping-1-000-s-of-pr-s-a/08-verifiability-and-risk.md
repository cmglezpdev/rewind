# 8. Verifiability and one-way doors

- The speakers distinguish reversible changes from one-way changes such as data loss. Poteto's answer is that autonomous merging depends on whether verification can provide enough confidence; strong verification can make some risky actions easier to reverse or trust. [`55:40`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MN9dGgmLyso&t=3340) [`56:30`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MN9dGgmLyso&t=3390)
- Domains that are difficult to verify programmatically are harder to automate safely. Poteto names verifiability as a key factor and says the industry still has to work out how to handle such domains. [`56:53`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MN9dGgmLyso&t=3413) [`57:30`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MN9dGgmLyso&t=3450)
- He points to formal proofs as one way to verify some software or mathematical work. Proof-oriented languages and tools such as Lean and TLA+ let people express properties and check whether cases such as race conditions have been covered. [`58:04`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MN9dGgmLyso&t=3484) [`58:34`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MN9dGgmLyso&t=3514)
- His criterion is practical: when an agent can demonstrate correctness through checks or proofs, merging becomes more defensible; when it cannot, automation should be limited by that uncertainty. [`58:58`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MN9dGgmLyso&t=3538)
