# 01 — Three Companies, Three Choices

## The framing numbers

- Netflix writes **3 million records per second** to its database ([`0:00`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XjHZCprrEgk&t=0))
- Instagram stored **over a billion users on PostgreSQL** for years ([`0:00`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XjHZCprrEgk&t=0))
- Twitter serves **300,000 timeline requests per second** from Redis ([`0:00`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XjHZCprrEgk&t=0))

## The central thesis

- No database is objectively "better" — each company has fundamentally **different access patterns** ([`0:00`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XjHZCprrEgk&t=0))
- The database that is perfect for one would be a **disaster** for another ([`0:25`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XjHZCprrEgk&t=25))

## What the video promises to deliver

- Not just *what* each company uses, but **why** they chose it ([`0:25`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XjHZCprrEgk&t=25))
- Which trade-offs they accepted, and what would break under a different choice ([`0:25`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XjHZCprrEgk&t=25))
- A reusable mental framework for startups and system design interviews ([`0:25`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XjHZCprrEgk&t=25))
