# Scope, spending, and agent tools

## What the title means in practice

- Theo describes extreme long-running experiments across seven Claude accounts. He frames the video as an account of his experiments and explicitly says it is not a guide viewers should replicate. [`1:48`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NBCM_ZoWZo8&t=108)
- His ordinary project prompts cost around $8 or less, sometimes $2. The experiments discussed here can consume hundreds of dollars per prompt and run for weeks, with compute sometimes costing more than model usage. [`0:52`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NBCM_ZoWZo8&t=52)
- Across these projects, he reports almost 4,000 aggregate agent-hours. This is accumulated time across agents, not one uninterrupted session. [`3:47`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NBCM_ZoWZo8&t=227)
- The opening claims are TS Rust compilation/build speedups of 4–10× depending on workload, and 100× or more for Lust on a subset of Rust. The later Lust discussion narrows its practical scope substantially. [`1:17`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NBCM_ZoWZo8&t=77) [`34:35`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NBCM_ZoWZo8&t=2075)

## Tools as a capability multiplier

- Theo's thesis is that sufficiently capable models benefit greatly from tools that let them gather information and act. He attributes improvements in personal agents partly to tool access rather than model intelligence alone. [`2:38`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NBCM_ZoWZo8&t=158)
- The Browserbase sponsorship illustrates browser access for searching, extracting HTML/JSON/Markdown, and navigating sites. These are sponsor claims, not comparative results established in the video. [`3:00`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NBCM_ZoWZo8&t=180)
- He says maintaining his own Puppeteer infrastructure involved provisioning costs, IP-related access failures, and browser bugs. [`3:22`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NBCM_ZoWZo8&t=202)
- The sponsored examples include CAPTCHA handling, uploads/downloads, and forms or pages that simple curl requests cannot reach. The broader lesson is to provision the interaction tools the task requires. [`3:47`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NBCM_ZoWZo8&t=227)
