# Why .NET-specific expertise loses value

- Mantinband argues that if agents can write code well in any language, there is no strong reason to become an expert specifically in .NET as though other languages and frameworks do not exist. [`7:57`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SCXhmMbbh6g&t=477)
- He says .NET is not special enough as a language/framework to justify disproportionate learning investment in a world where agents handle implementation details. [`8:34`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SCXhmMbbh6g&t=514)
- He believes writing and reading code are becoming less important as agent-assisted development matures. [`8:34`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SCXhmMbbh6g&t=514)
- Even nuanced areas such as hot paths, optimizations, and precision implementation details are areas where he says agents are becoming extremely strong. [`8:34`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SCXhmMbbh6g&t=514)
- The developer's likely role becomes writing clear rules, running tests, checking end-to-end behavior, and validating at a high level instead of manually mastering every .NET-specific detail. [`9:09`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SCXhmMbbh6g&t=549)
- He compares agent code generation to autonomous driving: once the automated system becomes statistically better, manual control can become the worse default even if it feels safer. [`10:12`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SCXhmMbbh6g&t=612)
- From a business perspective, he expects companies to reduce reliance on large numbers of implementation-focused developers once agents can produce higher-quality code at higher velocity. [`10:37`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SCXhmMbbh6g&t=637)
- He frames continuing to specialize mainly in implementation details as a poor strategic bet rather than a durable career path. [`11:03`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SCXhmMbbh6g&t=663)
