# Where agents still struggle

- The most important current agent weakness, in Mantinband's view, is handling huge amounts of context. [`2:58`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SCXhmMbbh6g&t=178)
- Even a 1 million token context window is not enough for a reliable grasp of a large production codebase. [`3:23`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SCXhmMbbh6g&t=203)
- Because an agent may miss whole parts of a codebase, Mantinband says developers cannot blindly trust it to produce the correct implementation for a plan. [`3:23`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SCXhmMbbh6g&t=203)
- He expects context handling to improve, but places it in the "humans have some time" category because larger context windows are expensive in memory and GPU usage. [`3:53`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SCXhmMbbh6g&t=233)
- He calls out codebases of roughly 300,000+ lines as a scale where context windows and compression remain a serious bottleneck. [`4:28`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SCXhmMbbh6g&t=268)
- His rough estimate is that this context advantage may remain meaningful for at least another one or two years, though he acknowledges hardware and tooling could change that. [`4:57`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SCXhmMbbh6g&t=297)
- He sees agent vision as a weaker source of human advantage because multimodal capabilities are improving quickly. [`4:57`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SCXhmMbbh6g&t=297) [`5:23`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SCXhmMbbh6g&t=323)
- He says agents remain bad at coming up with novel solutions, especially in complex domains where a simple original idea is needed. [`5:45`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SCXhmMbbh6g&t=345)
- The durable human advantage is not typing code but understanding the large product picture and forming novel architectural or domain ideas. [`7:25`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SCXhmMbbh6g&t=445)
