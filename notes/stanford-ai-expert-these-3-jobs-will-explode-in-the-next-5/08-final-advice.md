# 8. Final Advice

Eric acknowledges the anxiety directly: for juniors and people entering the workforce, "there's an understandable anxiety that a lot of people feel" [`12:35`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ci7ZDQ3m8cc&t=755).

## The trap to avoid

- **Do not evict yourself from the discipline** — concluding software is dead, it's cooked, "I shouldn't even be a part of this" [`12:47`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ci7ZDQ3m8cc&t=767).
- Eric's verdict on that conclusion: **"It's a wrong prediction to say that there's no hope for juniors at all in this new world"** [`13:10`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ci7ZDQ3m8cc&t=790).

## What juniors are uniquely poised to do

There is "absolutely a role and responsibility" juniors are uniquely poised to fill in an agentic, AI workforce, given [`12:47`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ci7ZDQ3m8cc&t=767):

- Being **really nimble**
- **Ability to adapt to new tools**
- Being **very interdisciplinary** — merging across multiple disciplines in interesting ways

- The caveat: it does require "a more thoughtful deliberation around where you fit into all of this," but the role is still crucial [`13:10`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ci7ZDQ3m8cc&t=790).

## The credibility check

- Eric puts his own hiring behind the claim: **"We're hiring here in my company, and … the right kind of junior folks I'll gladly bring them on"** [`13:10`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ci7ZDQ3m8cc&t=790).
- Note the qualifier — **"the right kind of"** — which points back to the skills in [05](05-become-interdisciplinary.md) and [06](06-the-top-1-percent-engineer.md), not to juniors generically.
