# 01 — Course Logistics & Agenda

## Housekeeping

- Legal notice: the instructor lacks Pennsylvania ACT 153 child-protection clearance, so nobody under 18 may be in the classroom ([`0:33`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=APqWIjtzNGE&t=33)).
- Course infrastructure ([`2:50`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=APqWIjtzNGE&t=170)):
  - Course web page — announcements, projects, homeworks.
  - Piazza — all discussion and questions.
  - Gradescope — homework/project submission and autograding.
  - Canvas — mid-semester and final grades.
  - Room cap is 140 ("before the Fire Marshall shows up"); 6 seats open at lecture time.
- Non-CMU viewers can access projects via a Gradescope code, but must not publish solutions on GitHub — a "Wall of Shame" is threatened for PRs containing solutions ([`3:30`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=APqWIjtzNGE&t=210)).
- Question policy: interrupt during lecture; the instructor refuses to answer lecture questions after class ([`5:09`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=APqWIjtzNGE&t=309)).

## Agenda for this lecture

1. Background — what database systems are ([`4:01`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=APqWIjtzNGE&t=241)).
2. The relational model and relational algebra — the bulk of the lecture, and the bedrock for most systems covered in the course ([`4:01`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=APqWIjtzNGE&t=241)).
3. Alternative data models — document/JSON and vector databases, treated as ancillary ([`4:23`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=APqWIjtzNGE&t=263)).
4. Open Q&A at the end ([`4:56`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=APqWIjtzNGE&t=296)).

## Framing

- The course's stated bias: SQL and the relational data model are the focus; core implementation ideas still transfer to other data models ([`4:23`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=APqWIjtzNGE&t=263)).
- Next lecture is "Modern SQL" — basic `SELECT *` is assumed knowledge; the lecture targets advanced features and where the SQL standard breaks down ([`1:12:18`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=APqWIjtzNGE&t=4338)).
