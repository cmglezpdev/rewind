Covers `0:00`–`5:46`. Martin's path from university into startups, before Rapportive and LinkedIn.

## Path overview

- Undergraduate computer science → first startup (didn't work out) → second startup (Rapportive) → sold to LinkedIn → moved to teaching distributed systems and writing DDIA → switched from industry back to academia during the writing of the book. [`2:52`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SVOrURyOu_U&t=172) [`3:20`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SVOrURyOu_U&t=200)

## First startup: GoTest.it (~2008)

- A cross-browser automated testing service for websites, built on Selenium and offered as a hosted service so customers wouldn't have to run their own VMs with different OS/browser combinations. Context: Internet Explorer was still big, Chrome had just come out, and browsers were mutually incompatible. [`3:20`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SVOrURyOu_U&t=200) [`3:47`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SVOrURyOu_U&t=227)
- It worked technically, but adoption was the hard part: prospects agreed cross-browser testing mattered in theory, but wouldn't integrate it into their workflow or invest in writing test scripts. [`4:20`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SVOrURyOu_U&t=260)
- Comparable companies from that era did survive — Sauce Labs succeeded — but even for them it was a slow-running, hard business. [`4:45`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SVOrURyOu_U&t=285)
- Built in the UK, mostly bootstrapped: Martin funded hires through consulting work, hired friends cheaply, with only a small amount of angel money. [`5:13`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SVOrURyOu_U&t=313) [`5:31`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SVOrURyOu_U&t=331)
