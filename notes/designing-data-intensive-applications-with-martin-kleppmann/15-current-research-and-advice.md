# 15 — Martin's current research and advice

Covers `1:18:32`–end. Two research areas, his personal stance on AI tools, and career advice.

## Research area 1: local-first software

- Collaborative software (like Google Docs, Figma) that better protects users' data: less dependent on a single cloud provider that can lock you out of your files, giving users greater agency and autonomy. ~10 years of work combining open source, algorithm development, and formal verification. [`1:18:32`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SVOrURyOu_U&t=4712) [`1:18:52`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SVOrURyOu_U&t=4732)

## Research area 2: cryptography for the physical world

- Brand new area: using cryptography to **prove things about the physical world**, focused on sustainability. [`1:18:52`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SVOrURyOu_U&t=4732)
- Problem 1 — carbon emissions: for emissions numbers to inform purchasing decisions they must be correct, but today incentives favor lying, creative accounting, and greenwashing. [`1:19:22`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SVOrURyOu_U&t=4762)
- Problem 2 — EU deforestation regulation: importers of coffee, cocoa, palm oil, etc. must prove which plot of land products came from and check against satellite imagery that it wasn't recently deforested. [`1:19:57`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SVOrURyOu_U&t=4797)
- The approach: cryptographic proofs about supply chains **without revealing commercially sensitive information** (e.g. who a company's suppliers are, which could leak a secret recipe) — prove the accounting was done correctly while keeping supplier/customer data private. [`1:19:57`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SVOrURyOu_U&t=4797) [`1:20:30`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SVOrURyOu_U&t=4830)

## Martin's own use of AI

- He writes very little code these days and hasn't personally needed AI agents much; sees the impact through collaborators using AI heavily for software development. [`1:20:58`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SVOrURyOu_U&t=4858)
- For the book's prose, he writes **every word by hand** — not on principle, but because *writing is how he figures things out*; the thinking is the goal. AI is productive for getting feedback on ideas and testing whether they hold up to scrutiny, in both industry and academia. [`1:21:25`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SVOrURyOu_U&t=4885) [`1:21:51`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SVOrURyOu_U&t=4911) [`1:22:14`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SVOrURyOu_U&t=4934)

## Career advice: industry vs. academia

- They're not mutually exclusive. Some of the best PhD students he's worked with spent a few years in industry first — real-world engineering experience gives breadth that straight-to-PhD students can lack. [`1:22:36`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SVOrURyOu_U&t=4956) [`1:23:18`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SVOrURyOu_U&t=4998)
- In the other direction, academia teaches nuanced, critical, first-principles thinking — an antidote to industry's "short-circuit reasoning" ("I heard this at a conference talk, I'll just go with that"). [`1:23:42`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SVOrURyOu_U&t=5022)
- Recommendation: weave in and out of industry and academia rather than treating them as exclusive career paths. [`1:24:09`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SVOrURyOu_U&t=5049)
