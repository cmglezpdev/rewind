# Supervised fine-tuning

## Why a pretrained model needs adaptation

- A base language model continues plausible text. Asked to explain the moon landing to a child, the lecture's GPT-3 example continues with another similar question instead of answering. [`59:56`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9vM4p9NN0Ts&t=3596) [`1:00:20`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9vM4p9NN0Ts&t=3620)
- Alignment or post-training aims to make the model follow instructions and reflect desired behavior, including answering helpfully and declining certain harmful requests. [`1:00:39`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9vM4p9NN0Ts&t=3639) [`1:01:02`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9vM4p9NN0Ts&t=3662) [`1:01:17`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9vM4p9NN0Ts&t=3677)
- Desired human-written question-answer data is scarce and expensive. Pretraining supplies broad language knowledge; fine-tuning then adapts the weights on a much smaller dataset of desired responses. [`1:01:42`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9vM4p9NN0Ts&t=3702) [`1:01:56`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9vM4p9NN0Ts&t=3716) [`1:02:07`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9vM4p9NN0Ts&t=3727)
- SFT uses the same next-token language-modeling loss as pretraining, applied to desired answers conditioned on instructions. Open Assistant is the lecture's example of collecting such human-written responses. [`1:02:27`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9vM4p9NN0Ts&t=3747) [`1:02:45`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9vM4p9NN0Ts&t=3765) [`1:03:16`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9vM4p9NN0Ts&t=3796) [`1:08:45`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9vM4p9NN0Ts&t=4125)

## Alpaca and data quantity

- Alpaca began with 175 human question-answer examples, prompted `text-davinci-003` to generate more, and collected 52,000 synthetic examples. Fine-tuning Llama 7B on those examples produced Alpaca 7B. [`1:04:11`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9vM4p9NN0Ts&t=3851) [`1:04:27`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9vM4p9NN0Ts&t=3867)
- The LIMA result as described here shows little improvement from scaling SFT examples from 2,000 to 32,000. The lecturer interprets this as selecting a response style already supported by pretraining, rather than teaching large amounts of new knowledge. This is a result and interpretation for the setting discussed. [`1:05:26`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9vM4p9NN0Ts&t=3926) [`1:05:47`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9vM4p9NN0Ts&t=3947) [`1:06:10`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9vM4p9NN0Ts&t=3970) [`1:06:30`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9vM4p9NN0Ts&t=3990)
- Purely recursive synthetic data may stop adding useful information. The lecturer proposes humans editing model drafts and supplying targeted feedback, but labels this intuition rather than strong empirical evidence. [`1:07:29`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9vM4p9NN0Ts&t=4049) [`1:07:45`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9vM4p9NN0Ts&t=4065) [`1:08:01`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9vM4p9NN0Ts&t=4081) [`1:08:20`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9vM4p9NN0Ts&t=4100)

## Why a small dataset can have a large effect

- Fine-tuning starts from pretrained weights, then trains on the post-training dataset. Its influence is not proportional to its fraction of all tokens ever seen because it is a later optimization phase. [`1:35:16`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9vM4p9NN0Ts&t=5716) [`1:35:35`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9vM4p9NN0Ts&t=5735) [`1:35:54`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9vM4p9NN0Ts&t=5754)
- Learning rate and training duration control how strongly those weights change. The lecturer cites a learning rate around \(10^{-5}\) and three epochs for Alpaca, while saying he does not know current industry schedules. [`1:09:30`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9vM4p9NN0Ts&t=4170) [`1:09:42`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9vM4p9NN0Ts&t=4182) [`1:36:25`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9vM4p9NN0Ts&t=5785) [`1:36:44`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9vM4p9NN0Ts&t=5804)
- The lecture describes full-weight fine-tuning in industry and LoRA as a cheaper alternative that adds trainable adjustments to layer outputs. [`1:34:05`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9vM4p9NN0Ts&t=5645) [`1:34:20`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9vM4p9NN0Ts&t=5660)
