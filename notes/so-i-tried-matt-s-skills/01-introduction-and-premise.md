# 01 — Introduction and premise

## What the video sets out to do

- Theo finally dives into a GitHub repo he had been eyeing for a long time: the AI skills collection published by **Matt Pocock** (whom he calls "the number one TypeScript YouTuber"). He admits he initially dismissed the skills, but Pocock kept refining them and kept them realistic ([`0:00`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0oXOOlqVu5M&t=0)).
- The claim that made it hard to ignore: a set of **small, simple markdown files** that, per Theo, racked up **over 200,000 GitHub stars**, putting it among the **10 most starred projects on all of GitHub** ([`0:30`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0oXOOlqVu5M&t=30)).
- Community pressure was part of the motivation — viewers had been asking for his honest take, and the "grill me" skill in particular had strong word of mouth ([`0:30`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0oXOOlqVu5M&t=30)).

## The method behind the video

- This is not a cold read: over the week before recording, Theo **installed a bunch of skills** from Pocock's repo and used them for real work ([`0:55`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0oXOOlqVu5M&t=55)).
- He audited the majority of skills in **both** suites, pulled his favorites into his own setup, and has been actively using them ([`1:21`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0oXOOlqVu5M&t=81)).

## The second collection: pstack

- Covering only Pocock's skills would shortchange the ecosystem — but Theo is blunt that **"the vast majority of these skill repos are absolute slop and should be ignored"** ([`1:21`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0oXOOlqVu5M&t=81)).
- The exception he's excited about is **pstack**, by **Lauren, a.k.a. "Potato"** — a former React core team member, now at Cursor, and one of the biggest contributors to the **React compiler** ([`1:21`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0oXOOlqVu5M&t=81), [`4:04`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0oXOOlqVu5M&t=244)).
- He flags pstack upfront as **"the underrated gem"** of the video ([`4:04`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0oXOOlqVu5M&t=244)).
