# Leaving Google to go all in on NeetCode

The decision to leave a fast promotion track, and the hard transition from employee to solo founder.

## The decision

- Freshly promoted to L4 with a clear path upward; his tech lead was "a bit perplexed" that he'd leave right after a fast promotion [`25:28`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=1528), [`26:11`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=1571)
- Deciding factors: the opportunity window ("maybe that opportunity isn't going to be there forever") and Google's safety net — leavers in good standing can usually return within a year [`26:26`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=1586)
- He liked Google itself — the people, the non-stressful environment — which made the choice harder [`25:47`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=1547)

## The transition

- Hardest part of leaving: the tooling. Google's internal tools are so good that adjusting to GitHub (he dislikes its UX) was painful; several companies exist just to commercialize internal Google tools (e.g. Cockroach Labs) [`27:25`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=1645), [`27:48`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=1668)
- Building and managing a team was the real struggle — he only became comfortable delegating and managing in the last ~6 months (years in): hiring, letting people go, learning what motivates people. "They're not like agents where you just give them the task" [`28:11`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=1691), [`28:32`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=1712)
- Now he loves leadership: an effective leader "can make a magnitude of difference" even in a small team — visible at CEO scale when a company pivots direction under new leadership [`28:51`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=1731), [`29:09`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=1749)

## Early tech choices and the shortcut philosophy

- Original stack chosen while still at Google: Google Cloud + Firebase (easy to use), Angular (what he used at work) — he regrets both, but LLMs have made migrations "relatively trivial" [`29:49`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=1789), [`30:09`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=1809)
- Building the app was never the interesting part: "nobody's going to care that much about the performance of my site or the tech stack" — they care about UX and whether the video explains things well. The education is the product [`30:09`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=1809), [`30:44`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=1844)
- Business value exceeded bad tech choices — which taught him to prioritize what matters and take shortcuts on what doesn't, similar to Elon Musk's process of cutting steps and reintroducing only what proves necessary [`30:44`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=1844), [`31:13`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=1873)
