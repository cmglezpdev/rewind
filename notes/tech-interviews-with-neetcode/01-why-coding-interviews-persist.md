# Why coding interviews haven't died

Despite years of predictions that AI would kill coding interviews, the DSA (data structures & algorithms) format has stayed remarkably consistent at big tech companies.

## Navi's explanation for the stickiness

- Coding has changed dramatically, but the interview format is "the one area that have surprisingly stayed pretty consistent" — companies are only experimenting mildly with AI-assisted interviews [`3:19`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=199)
- DSA interviews were never meant to test job-relevant skills; they evaluate whether "somebody can think" [`4:23`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=263)
- Second reason: companies have no idea how to evaluate candidates — and probably never did. Amazon ran internal studies showing that no matter how much interview data you collect, predicting on-the-job performance is very hard [`4:46`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=286)
- Even a technically good hire may fail for reasons interviews can't measure: motivation, team fit, environment [`5:09`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=309)

## Why change is so slow at big companies

- "If it ain't broke, don't fix it": any change risks making the process worse [`5:18`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=318)
- Changing the process is bureaucratic and requires retraining thousands of interviewers — most of whom hate interviewing. Meta's AI coding interview experiments show how hard the training is [`5:32`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=332)
- Big companies need a standardized process; new evaluation formats add variables (AI prompts to check, different training) that make consistency "practically impossible" to measure [`5:49`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=349)
- Prediction: new formats will appear, but the transition will be much slower than most people expect [`6:15`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=375)
