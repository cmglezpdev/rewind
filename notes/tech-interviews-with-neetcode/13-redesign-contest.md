# The NeetCode redesign contest

A $2,500 contest for redesigning the NeetCode site became an accidental experiment in AI-generated low-effort work.

## What happened

- Navi offered **$2,500** to whoever submitted the winning redesign; results so far: "a little bit disappointing" — practically all submissions were obviously AI-generated (which he says is fine in itself) [`57:57`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=3477)
- The failure mode: when asked *why* they moved/removed buttons or content and what the pros and cons were, submitters couldn't answer, or gave vague answers. "Me looking at their site for 5 minutes, I can articulate things about their design better than they can" [`58:20`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=3500), [`58:44`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=3524)
- His diagnosis: not intelligence — effort, caring, and skill set [`58:59`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=3539)
- His view of UX: it's not prettiness; nobody cares how pretty a site is if they don't understand what it's for or what value it gives them [`59:15`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=3555), [`59:37`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=3577)

## The incentive problem (host's counterpoint)

- A contest flips incentives: with N contestants, expected value per submission drops (10 contestants → ~$250 expected), so rational effort is one AI prompt [`59:37`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=3577), [`59:55`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=3595)
- Navi's lesson learned: he should have picked a small pool of people, paid them up front, and chosen from that work [`1:00:22`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=3622)

## The deeper point

- Pen and paper + articulated intent would beat a raw prompt: on his own site, even AI-coded parts, he can justify every positioning choice with metrics ("this is used the most, so I want to make this prominent") [`1:00:45`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=3645), [`1:01:05`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=3665)
- Low-effort prompting produces low-effort output — the differentiator is being able to defend your choices [`1:01:27`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=3687)
