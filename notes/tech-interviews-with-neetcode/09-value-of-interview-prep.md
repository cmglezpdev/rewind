# The value of coding interview prep

What DSA preparation actually gives you beyond the algorithms themselves.

- Navi's own case: after a year of only making NeetCode videos (little real development), he still got promoted quickly at Google despite being "subpar" at raw coding compared to peers [`39:46`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=2386), [`40:08`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=2408)
- What transferred from prep to the job:
  - Facing a hard, unfamiliar problem with unfamiliar internal tools, he could sit down, work through it, and make a plan [`40:35`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=2435)
  - Communication, trained by making explanation videos: telling his manager "this is the approach I'm thinking… just so you're on the same page" — exactly like narrating an interview solution [`40:56`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=2456)
  - Trade-off thinking: "In engineering, there's no correct answer like there is in math or science. Engineering is about the best solution at the time" [`41:15`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=2475)
- The mistake people make now: obsessing over hard skills ("can I write this loop, do I know this data structure") while forgetting to zoom out to what engineering is about [`41:42`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=2502)
- What great engineers who hop domains actually carry isn't a specific language skill — it's what you gain *from* the learning, like education in general: 20 years of subjects mold you in ways that are hard to articulate but clearly real [`42:07`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=2527), [`42:30`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=2550)
