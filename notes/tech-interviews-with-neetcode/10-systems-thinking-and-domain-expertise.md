# Systems thinking and domain expertise

A debate on what durable skill survives AI: abstract systems thinking vs. deep domain knowledge.

## Navi's position: systems thinking endures

- From a conversation with Chip Heath: it's impossible to predict which *hard* skills will matter (which language, how high-level, whether to code at all), but **systems thinking** — a broad concept spanning engineering and other disciplines — clearly will [`43:22`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=2602)
- Construction analogy: workers do the individual tasks, but *somebody designed the system* — the procedures, checks, and verification that let a building too complex for any one person get built. There's no course for that skill, and it's where the value comes from [`43:48`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=2628), [`44:16`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=2656), [`44:36`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=2676)

## Gergely's pushback: it's really domain expertise

- Systems don't exist in a vacuum: agriculture, legal (country-specific), healthcare (US vs UK vs Romania), payments — great "systems thinkers" in a domain are often just deep domain experts, and the two overlap [`45:03`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=2703)
- Knowing multiple domains may be what builds abstract systems-thinking ability [`45:54`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=2754)

## Convergence

- Navi agrees domain details matter, but points to engineers you'd trust to move from payments to real estate: they learn faster and perform better — and that's not innate, it comes from somewhere [`46:18`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=2778), [`46:38`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=2798)
- The wrong mindset: "it was a waste of time to learn this subject because I didn't use those details on the job" — which is exactly what people now say about AI making fundamentals obsolete. Going deep on hard things is never wasted [`46:38`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=2798), [`47:06`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=2826)
