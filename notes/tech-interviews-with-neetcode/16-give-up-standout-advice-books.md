# "Maybe some people should just give up", standout advice, and book recommendation

The closing stretch: clarifying the most contentious take, career advice, and inspirations.

## The "give up" hot take, clarified

- He does *not* encourage giving up — the point is: if your attitude is not wanting to try hard, do things yourself, or dig deeper, "you should know what you're getting yourself into". Many cheat through college degrees and expect a job at the end [`1:22:49`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=4969), [`1:23:11`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=4991)
- Reaction to the video: some were angry, most agreed with the substance (while noting he could have been nicer). Many admitted over-prompting had made them worse and less happy, and the video pushed them to refocus on learning [`1:23:35`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=5015), [`1:23:54`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=5034)
- He left the video up despite a possible reputation hit, because nobody else was saying it [`1:24:13`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=5053), [`1:24:26`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=5066)

## How to be a standout engineer

- Know your audience — even in interviews you're talking to a person, not taking a standardized test [`1:25:04`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=5104)
- On a team: know the people around you and what they care about; ask questions instead of assuming; then "go very hard in the direction that you think is correct" and iterate on the feedback loop [`1:25:28`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=5128), [`1:25:52`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=5152)
- His signature move: telling every manager "if you have feedback for me, please tell me. I will not get offended" — managers were consistently surprised and read it as genuine drive [`1:25:52`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=5152), [`1:26:20`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=5180)
- Recurring theme: effort. Take shortcuts only for business outcomes; otherwise put in the work. Simple on paper, hard in practice — Navi admits he personally hates change and it takes him years [`1:26:41`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=5201), [`1:27:04`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=5224)
- Soft skills were the reason he got promoted and was liked: "it's important to be likable" [`1:27:24`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=5244), [`1:27:42`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=5262)

## Book recommendation and inspirations

- Huge fan of **Martin Fowler** and his book — because he goes deep and ends every chapter with a hundred references, feeding the "always one more follow-up question" mindset [`1:27:55`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=5275)
- Relates more to scientists, PhDs, and researchers than to engineers; deliberately identifies admirable qualities in people and imitates them [`1:27:55`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=5275), [`1:28:19`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=5299)

## Host's closing synthesis

- Navi runs one of the biggest coding-prep sites yet hires for skills outside coding: motivation, explaining your own thinking, agency [`1:28:41`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=5321)
- The LeetCode interview survived "not because it predicts job performance — it just doesn't — but because nobody has found anything better that scales" [`1:29:05`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=5345)
- Effort is the new differentiator: "anyone can prompt a design, a feature, or an answer, but what you cannot prompt is caring and your ability to defend your choices" [`1:29:05`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xafwfGVBxos&t=5345)
