# 1. Why a `/teach` skill

## Origin

- Matt Pocock has been teaching for 10 years: 6 years as a voice coach, 4 years teaching developers ([`0:00`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s5T5oQJcJ6U&t=0)).
- The long-standing idea: encode everything he knows about teaching into a **skill**, so anyone could learn anything with it ([`0:00`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s5T5oQJcJ6U&t=0)).
- The skill was written in a single sitting — during a bus ride to London — and turned out to work well ([`0:00`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s5T5oQJcJ6U&t=0)).

## The proof case: the Rubik's Cube

- The first thing the skill taught him was **how to solve a Rubik's Cube** — something he'd always wanted to learn but never had the time or inclination for ([`0:20`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s5T5oQJcJ6U&t=20)).
- What made it work, in his words: it felt like a real teacher who
  - taught the way he likes to be taught, and
  - was totally aligned with his mission ([`0:20`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s5T5oQJcJ6U&t=20)).

> The Rubik's Cube run is the running example throughout the whole video — every workspace file shown belongs to that session.
