# 9. Use cases, and developers as AI first movers

## Engineering use case: onboarding to a codebase

The strongest professional application he names ([`10:04`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s5T5oQJcJ6U&t=604)):

- **Why written documentation fails at onboarding:**
  - it's painful to produce and must be kept up to date ([`10:04`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s5T5oQJcJ6U&t=604));
  - it's almost certainly **outside the reader's zone of proximal development** — it can't be right for everyone at once ([`10:04`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s5T5oQJcJ6U&t=604)).
  - Concrete mismatch cases: someone who knows your stack but not your problem domain, versus someone who knows the domain but has never used TypeScript ([`10:04`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s5T5oQJcJ6U&t=604)).
- **The `teach` shape instead:** give the new hire their own workspace, point it at the codebase, and let them learn independently how to work with the code and what the concepts are — *"a productive employee in record time"* ([`10:30`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s5T5oQJcJ6U&t=630)).

## The closing argument: developers are the first movers

- **AI is currently better at writing code than at almost anything else** — a few exceptions aside ([`11:07`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s5T5oQJcJ6U&t=667)).
- Therefore developers hold an accidental advantage: they are the first group to test AI on a problem it is genuinely good at, and so the first to really **get to know AI** and build things with it ([`11:07`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s5T5oQJcJ6U&t=667)).
- The move that follows: **take the ideas developed in coding, turn them into skills, and bring them out to the world** — `teach` is his first phase of exactly that ([`11:44`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s5T5oQJcJ6U&t=704)).
- The morale note for frustrating sessions with Claude Code or Codex: the instincts you're building working with AI now transfer **outside coding domains**, and that will be valuable whatever the future of work looks like ([`11:44`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s5T5oQJcJ6U&t=704), [`12:14`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s5T5oQJcJ6U&t=734)).
