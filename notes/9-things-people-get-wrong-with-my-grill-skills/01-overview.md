# 1. Overview — what the grill skills are for

← [Back to README](videos-notes/9-things-people-get-wrong-with-my-grill-skills/README.md) · Next: [Low vs high fidelity questions](02-low-vs-high-fidelity-questions.md)

## What the skills do

- `Grill Me` and `Grill with Docs` are used worldwide as a **replacement for plan mode** in agents ([`0:00`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UzMNBN6xLLA&t=0)).
- Their design intent: **relentlessly question you** until you and the agent reach a *shared understanding* of the thing to be built ([`0:00`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UzMNBN6xLLA&t=0)).
- Common complaint the author winces at: *"Codex just asked me 200 questions"* — treated as a symptom of misuse, not a bug in the skill ([`0:00`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UzMNBN6xLLA&t=0)).

## Why the failure modes are user-side

- The skills' quality **depends on the skill of the person answering** ([`0:25`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UzMNBN6xLLA&t=25)).
- To use them well, you must be good at planning, which means you need:
  - an understanding of **scope** ([`0:25`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UzMNBN6xLLA&t=25));
  - a sense of **what level of fidelity a given question requires** to be answered ([`0:25`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UzMNBN6xLLA&t=25)).
- The skills themselves are **short** files — they are designed to *aid* an engineer, not *replace* one ([`0:25`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UzMNBN6xLLA&t=25)).

## Structure of the video

- Nine things people get wrong, but framed first through a set of **lenses** for understanding the failure modes — without the right framing you cannot change the behaviour ([`0:53`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UzMNBN6xLLA&t=53)).

The lenses that follow:

| Lens | Failure it explains |
|---|---|
| Question fidelity | Trying to answer ungrillable questions in a grilling session |
| Scope | Hidden high-fidelity questions + hitting the model's dumb zone |
| Active vs passive | 540-question interviews, or endless grilling instead of coding |
| Context value | Throwing away the session's design decisions |
| Model choice | Using a model too weak for parametric-knowledge work |
| Parallelism | Leaving throughput on the table |
