# 6. What Separates Energizing Use From Unhealthy Use

*Chapter 5 of the video ([`14:00`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPUn1Fnfn0k&t=844))*

Aaron Francis is included precisely as a **counter-case**: he reached out to say he gets no negative mental health side effects from AI coding ([`14:04`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPUn1Fnfn0k&t=844)).

## He still went through the "psychosis phase"

- What it felt like: **infinite possibility** — "I could literally do anything" — and that part was scary ([`14:32`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPUn1Fnfn0k&t=872)).
- November–December–January: ripping tokens until midnight or 2 a.m., with a spouse and four kids at home ([`14:32`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPUn1Fnfn0k&t=872)).
- He did **not** burn out on it ([`15:04`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPUn1Fnfn0k&t=904)).

## What defused it: a change in the shape of ambition

The realization ([`15:04`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPUn1Fnfn0k&t=904), [`15:44`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPUn1Fnfn0k&t=944)):

- It's **less of a gold rush** than it looked, because every other developer has the same power.
- Original plan: build 40 products with "god-like power." Revised plan: **one or two things with more vertical ambition**.
- Generic output (to-do lists, screen-recorder clones) is what everyone else is also producing — no moat.
- **Broad → deep**: depth creates a moat, and a moat kills the urgency. "I can just go to bed because I'm not going to see it launch on Twitter tomorrow."

## Agent count vs. outcomes

Distribution of agents run simultaneously ([`15:44`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPUn1Fnfn0k&t=944), [`16:13`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPUn1Fnfn0k&t=973)):

- Roughly **two in three** run just one or two agents.
- Next most common: three agents.
- Fewer than 200 respondents run four or more.

As agent count rises ([`16:28`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPUn1Fnfn0k&t=988)):

| Metric | Direction |
|---|---|
| Enjoyment of coding | ⬆ up |
| Moderate-or-greater sleep disturbance | ⬆ up |
| Feeling that skills are diminishing | ⬇ down |

Two competing readings offered for the skill result, with no verdict ([`17:03`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPUn1Fnfn0k&t=1023)):

1. These people aren't losing skills because they're more deeply involved.
2. They've lost the most and can no longer tell.

**Stated caveat:** the data for four or five agents is thin; more respondents are needed before drawing conclusions ([`17:03`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPUn1Fnfn0k&t=1023)).

## Miranda Heath — it depends what you get out of the work

- People get different things out of development work, and AI coding can make the job harder and more burnout-inducing, or easier, depending on which ([`17:03`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPUn1Fnfn0k&t=1023)).
- If what you enjoy is the **intellectual challenge and the thinking parts** rather than the produce-something parts, AI can change the nature of the work enough that you **lose your love for it** ([`17:35`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPUn1Fnfn0k&t=1055)).
