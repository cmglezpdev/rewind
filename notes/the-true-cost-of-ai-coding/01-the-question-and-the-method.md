# 1. The Question and the Method

## The trigger

- The video opens with the release of Anthropic's Fable model, hype at an all-time high, and a claim that the US government had banned it, blocking use after a certain date ([`0:00`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPUn1Fnfn0k&t=0)).
- A developer named Rob stayed up all night trying to burn through all his tokens before the model went offline, and ended up in the hospital. The tweet about it reached 4.7 million impressions ([`0:21`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPUn1Fnfn0k&t=21)).
- Most replies were confusion rather than support: how does prompting put someone in a hospital? ([`0:21`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPUn1Fnfn0k&t=21)).

## The investigation

Host: Scott Tolinski (Syntax) ([`1:00`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPUn1Fnfn0k&t=60)).

Two sources of evidence, combined throughout the video:

1. **Interviews** with mental health and industry experts ([`0:21`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPUn1Fnfn0k&t=21)).
2. **A survey** of nearly 1,300 developers worldwide, collected in one week ([`0:21`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPUn1Fnfn0k&t=21), [`4:56`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPUn1Fnfn0k&t=296)).

## Stated methodological limits

- The survey is **self-selected**: results reflect the people who chose to answer, not the developer population as a whole ([`4:56`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPUn1Fnfn0k&t=296)).
- The survey was still open and still collecting data at publication time ([`5:19`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPUn1Fnfn0k&t=319), [`23:26`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPUn1Fnfn0k&t=1406)).
- Correlations are explicitly **not** presented as causation — the relationships "showed up together in the data" and could run either way ([`6:23`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPUn1Fnfn0k&t=383)).
- Skill and enjoyment questions measure **self-perception**, not assessed ability ([`11:19`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPUn1Fnfn0k&t=679)).
