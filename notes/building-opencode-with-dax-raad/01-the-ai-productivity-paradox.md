# 01 — The AI Productivity Paradox

Dax opens the interview with a paradox: he co-built one of the most-used AI coding tools, uses it daily, and still doesn't feel meaningfully faster.

## The core tension

- OpenCode was built for the team's own use — "the easiest products to build are ones that you can use yourself" — and it is a critical part of their workflows [`3:23`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=203).
- Yet the old problems remain: "I'm working as hard as I ever have. I'm struggling as much as I ever have" — objectively things got easier, but the thinking load hasn't dropped [`3:46`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=226).
- No competitor in the coding-agent space — where everyone is maximally into AI — is crushing the others through better AI usage. If AI were a decisive advantage, this space would show the gap, and it doesn't [`6:28`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=388).

## Why speed doesn't translate into better products

- Impact of AI differs by company lifecycle: pre-product-market-fit, at PMF (OpenCode's stage), and decade-old PMF companies. Pre-PMF, AI barely helps because the job is figuring out *what* to do — "it's better to think a lot instead of swinging a lot" [`4:37`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=277).
- At PMF the temptation is 1:1 reactivity: user problem → prompt the agent, competitor feature → prompt the agent. Shipping a thousand features that way "actually adds up to a horrible product" — nothing cohesive, a Frankenstein [`5:18`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=318).
- Shipping is a one-way door: "The moment you ship something, you're stuck supporting it forever" — every future feature interacts with it, so restraint still matters [`5:41`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=341).
- Being able to ship 10× more doesn't mean having 10× as many good ideas. Dax's current struggle is how to *slow everyone down* [`6:04`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=364).
- The last 6 months OpenCode operated very differently from before; a lot went wrong, and they're now pulling back to see what from the "old world" still makes sense [`6:28`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=388).
