# 03 — Open Source: SST and OpenNext

## Getting into open source

- After Ride Health, Dax joined a Series-B startup as a director — his first full-time management role with no programming tasks. With 3–4 hours of daily meetings, he filled the rest exploring open source [`13:16`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=796).
- He found SST (a toolkit for full-stack apps on AWS, by Frank and Jay) a couple of months after launch, contributed, invested in their round, then joined a month later — they returned the investment as salary, on which he then owed taxes. His advice: "if you're going to invest in a company, make sure you're not going to join them" [`14:02`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=842).

## OpenNext — the project that blew them up

- Born from a year of users nagging: people wanted help deploying Next.js on AWS. Nobody on the team wanted the work (complex framework, JS bundling internals, tedious), so "we made Frank do all of it" [`14:46`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=886).
- Design goal from day one: **the project shouldn't need to exist**. It filled a gap left because the Next.js team naturally focused on Vercel; the ideal end state was Vercel/Next.js absorbing it [`15:15`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=915).
- It annoyed Vercel but rallied other providers with the same problem — Cloudflare, Netlify, eventually Microsoft and Google — who built adapters. The Next.js team eventually shipped an official adapters API, the effort became collaborative, and the need for OpenNext is fading [`15:38`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=938).

> This "pick a temporary bad guy, galvanize their competitors" pattern becomes OpenCode's core strategic playbook — see [04](04-opencode-origin-and-growth.md) and [05](05-anthropic-ban-and-the-counter-move.md).
