# 13 — Work Setup, the Role of Engineers, Advice, and Books

## Dax's work setup

- Framework desktop running Arch Linux, one 5K display, a tiling window manager he's used for 10 years; Shure SM7B mic, iPhone as camera [`1:11:32`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=4292).
- All real work happens on a remote machine: SSH into a beefier computer, tmux sessions (one per project), Neovim as editor — screen usually split with Neovim on the left and OpenCode on the right [`1:11:57`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=4317).

## The role of engineers with AI: an old problem in new clothes

- The emerging framing: if engineers don't write the code, their role is building guardrails — testing story, conventions, patterns — so anyone (another engineer, even marketing) can prompt an agent and safely ship [`1:13:02`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=4382), [`1:13:22`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=4402).
- Dax's twist: **that's not novel.** It's the eternal problem of getting junior engineers to ship safely and making less-experienced people punch above their weight — patterns, tests, codebases that are easy to work in [`1:13:47`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=4427), [`1:14:07`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=4447).
- "The coding agents are a bunch of idiots and they are going to work 24/7 and ship a lot of stuff — so you need way more guardrails than you used to" [`1:14:47`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=4487).
- Consequence: old "boring enterprisey" patterns are coming back. OpenCode always did light domain-driven design; they're now doing it much more heavily. Verbose patterns produced reliable, modular, safe code — the verbosity was the downside, and "you're not typing it out anymore," so you get the benefits without the cost [`1:14:47`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=4487), [`1:15:07`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=4507).
- Same logic for 2000s-era design patterns: they were training wheels good programmers didn't need — "but the agents don't have training wheels, so you got to put them back on" [`1:15:27`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=4527), [`1:15:51`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=4551).

## Advice for experienced engineers

- Software engineering alone is a fine career, but **engineer + deep industry expertise is "a deadly combo"**: know farming well and be a decent engineer and you're one of the top 10 people in the world for that combination [`1:16:12`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=4572), [`1:16:42`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=4602).
- Unique advantage of the profession: you can switch industries — do 10 years in medicine, decide you don't like it, and become an expert somewhere else [`1:17:05`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=4625).
- A year inside any industry puts you ahead of ~99% of people on it; the trap is turning your brain off and only doing the assigned UI task instead of learning the business [`1:17:42`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=4662).

## Book recommendation

- Dax barely reads — his wife reads avidly and he "restates the good ideas like they're his own" [`1:18:12`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=4692).
- Standing recommendation: **Nassim Taleb** — *Skin in the Game*, *The Black Swan*. A book is "one good idea inflated into a book," but these are fundamental truths you see play out everywhere [`1:18:37`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=4717).
- The idea that shaped him most: **emergent properties** — almost everything great (robust software, walkable cities, disease-resistant organisms) comes from bottom-up interaction of smaller entities, not top-down design. "Once you understand his ideas, you realize they underpin the whole world" [`1:19:00`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=4740), [`1:19:36`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=4776).
