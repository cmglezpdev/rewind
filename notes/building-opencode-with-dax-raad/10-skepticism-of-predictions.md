# 10 — Skepticism of Predictions

## The "24 to 29-year-old engineer" tweet

- The viral claim: engineers aged 24–29 will become "the most valuable asset in technology" because they have pre-AI principles and post-AI speed. Dax called BS [`55:41`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=3341).
- His diagnosis of the genre: it's the classic "person like me has all the advantages, person not like me has all the disadvantages" post. Note the range is 24–29, not 18–25 — because the author falls in it [`56:27`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=3387), [`57:34`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=3454).

## Why predictions are mostly psychology

- Root cause: "we are experiencing a moment of great change, everyone is nervous about what that means for their own position, and a defense mechanism is to confidently assert a future in which you're a winner. That's almost what every single prediction is" [`56:27`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=3387).
- The pattern always traces down to: my company will succeed, others will fail; my job won't be replaced, everyone else's will [`56:51`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=3411).
- Dax's alternative: focus on the next day. "I didn't know I was going to be working on this a year ago. I don't know what I'm going to be working on a year from now" [`57:16`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=3436).
- Young vs. experienced is the eternal trade — energy without experience vs. experience without 20-year-old energy — nothing new about it [`57:34`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=3454).

## On the pace of change

- Industry veterans (Kent Beck, Martin Fowler, Grady Booch) say they've seen shifts like mainframes → microprocessors, but spread over a decade rather than a couple of years — this one is faster [`58:17`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=3497).
- Dax: outcomes are always counterintuitive — "none of the obvious things ever happen… it's going to be so weird from our point of view now, and make total sense in hindsight. Predicting is a lot harder than people think" [`58:38`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1VqKUrxR2C8&t=3518).
